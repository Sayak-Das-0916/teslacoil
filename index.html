1. Introduction

A Tesla Coil is an electrical device used to produce very high voltage, low current, and high frequency alternating current (AC). It was invented by Nikola Tesla in 1891.

2. Objectives of the Project

  • To study the construction and working of a Tesla Coil
  • To understand high-voltage and high-frequency AC
  • To learn electrical resonance and electromagnetic induction
  • To demonstrate wireless transmission of energy
  • To study applications, advantages, and limitations

3. History and Background

Nikola Tesla was a pioneer in electrical engineering and contributed greatly to AC systems. He believed electrical energy could be transmitted wirelessly.

4. Principle of Operation

a) Electromagnetic Induction

An alternating current in the primary coil produces a changing magnetic field, which induces voltage in the secondary coil.

b) Electrical Resonance

Both coils are tuned to the same resonant frequency, allowing maximum energy transfer.

c) High-Frequency AC

High frequency allows efficient energy transfer through air.

6. Working of Tesla Coil

  1. The power supply charges the capacitor
  2. Spark gap conducts
  3. Primary LC circuit oscillates
  4. Magnetic field induces voltage
  5. Resonance increases voltage
  6. Sparks appear at top load

7. Circuit Diagram and Explanation

8. Types of Tesla Coils

  • Spark Gap Tesla Coil (SGTC)
  • Solid State Tesla Coil (SSTC)
  • Dual Resonant SSTC (DRSSTC)

9. Applications of Tesla Coil

  • High-voltage demonstrations
  • Educational purposes
  • Wireless power experiments
  • Radio research

10. Advantages

  • Produces extremely high voltage
  • Wireless energy transfer demonstration
  • Simple basic construction

11. Limitations

  • Low efficiency
  • High energy loss
  • Dangerous voltage

12. Safety Precautions

  • Proper insulation
  • Safe operating distance
  • Protective equipment
  • Expert supervision

13. Future Scope

  • Wireless charging
  • Resonant energy transfer
  • High-frequency electronics

14. Conclusion

The Tesla Coil remains an important educational device demonstrating high-frequency AC and electrical resonance, inspiring future scientific research.
